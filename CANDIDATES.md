# Candidate judging

Gate used for every candidate: one English sentence, UTF-8 text, 1-200 characters, not only whitespace.

Judge criteria, in order: buried terms-of-service fit; standalone funniness; legal-boilerplate register until the absurdity hits; originality and concrete specificity; tightness with no self-explaining joke.

## Candidates

- A (120 chars): Any dispute arising under this Section shall be resolved by a coin toss conducted by a notary who has never seen a coin.
- B (131 chars): The Company may, at its sole discretion, replace any portion of the Service with a smaller, more judgmental portion of the Service.
- C (143 chars): You acknowledge that the Service may from time to time emit a low, disappointed hum, which shall not constitute a breach, warranty, or opinion.
- D (140 chars): Any feedback you provide becomes the property of the Company, including feedback provided silently while staring at the screen in disbelief.
- E (178 chars): If any provision of these Terms is held unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force, and the unenforceable provision shall be read aloud to a houseplant.
- F (145 chars): The Company reserves the right to modify these Terms at any time, including retroactively, prospectively, and during your dreams, without notice.
- G (146 chars): You agree not to reverse engineer the Service, except for the parts that reverse engineer you first, which you may admire quietly from a distance.
- H (140 chars): Notices under this Agreement shall be deemed delivered only by email, courier, or a solemn nod from a raccoon wearing the Company's lanyard.

## Pairwise bracket

- A beats G: A stays inside dispute-resolution boilerplate until the impossible notary procedure; G is clever but more meta and explains its turn.
- D beats C: D's silent-feedback image lands faster and is more specific to a feedback-ownership clause; C is deadpan but subtler.
- E beats F: E uses classic severability language and a concrete houseplant image; F is funny but broader and less visual.
- H beats B: H has a concrete raccoon-and-lanyard image inside a notice-delivery clause; B's "more judgmental" turn is comparatively abstract.
- A beats D: D is relatable, but A's notary who has never seen a coin creates a sharper impossible legal procedure.
- H beats E: E is very terms-like, but H is tighter and the solemn raccoon nod is funnier on first reading.
- H beats A: both hide in boilerplate, but H's absurdity is more concrete and visual, while A's paradox takes a beat longer to resolve.

## Selected entry
